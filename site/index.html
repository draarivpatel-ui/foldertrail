Why now

BaseCamp still works for many people today. Here is what is known about its future on the Mac.

  • Garmin isn’t planning new versions

    In September 2026, a Garmin staff member wrote on Garmin’s forum that “no further updates or versions are planned at this time.”

    Source: Garmin Forums, “Basecamp upgrade for Apple Silicon”, 21 September 2026.

  • macOS 28 stops it on Apple-silicon Macs

    BaseCamp for Mac is an Intel app. Macs with Apple silicon (M1 and later) run it through Apple’s Rosetta. Apple says that starting with macOS 28, Rosetta will be available only for certain older games. So once one of these Macs moves to macOS 28, the next major version (expected in 2027), BaseCamp won’t open on it.

    Source: Apple Support, “Using Intel-based apps on a Mac with Apple silicon” (article 102527).

  • Intel Macs and macOS 27 keep working

    On a Mac with an Intel processor, or an Apple-silicon Mac that stays on macOS 27 or earlier, BaseCamp keeps running. You don’t have to rush.

  • BaseCamp’s own exports lose your folders

    BaseCamp can save GPX files, but GPX has no folders, so the folders and lists you built don’t come along. You can export one list at a time while BaseCamp still opens, then rebuild the folders by hand. Foldertrail reads the library itself, where your folders are kept.

    Source: posts on Garmin’s BaseCamp forums, 2012 to 2026.

What you get

One ZIP file. Unzip it with a double-click, and you have:

  • GPX files in your folders

    One GPX file for each BaseCamp list, inside folders named like yours. Most map apps and Garmin’s own tools import GPX. If an app prefers single items, you can choose one file per waypoint, track or route instead.

  • A KML file with the whole tree

    One file that keeps every folder and list, for Google Earth and other apps that show KML folders. It’s optional.

  • A report

    What converted, what didn’t, and why, by name. It comes as a web page and as a text file.

An item that sits in several lists is written into each of those lists’ files. The guide explains how to load the files into Garmin Explore, Gaia GPS, CalTopo and others.

Read the privacy page

  • Nothing is uploaded

    Your waypoints, tracks, routes, notes and folder names never leave your computer.

  • One request, for the license

    When you unlock the download, the page sends your license key and Foldertrail’s product ID to the payment provider to check the key. A saved key is checked again at most once per browser session, when you open the converter. It’s the only request the page makes to another site.

  • No analytics, no cookies

    No tracking, no ads, nothing loaded from other sites. An accepted license key is kept in your browser so you don’t have to paste it again.

Limits

What Foldertrail doesn’t do, so you know before you start. The report lists anything that applies to your library.

  • Routes are straight lines

    Routes come out as straight lines between their via points. Routes that BaseCamp calculated along roads aren’t converted in this version, and neither is a route whose via points can’t all be found: the preview and the report list them by name.

  • Some data stays behind

    BirdsEye imagery, custom maps, Adventures, geocache logs and photos aren’t converted. When Foldertrail finds some of these in your library, such as BirdsEye imagery, the preview and the report mention them.

  • Track elevation and time only when they read cleanly

    If they don’t, the track keeps its shape without them, and the report says so.

  • Still being tested

    Foldertrail has been checked against real libraries made in BaseCamp 4.8.13 for Mac (with made-up content) and against many made-up libraries built for testing. Yours may hold something we haven’t seen yet, so before you buy, check the free preview against your library: in BaseCamp if it still opens, or as you remember it.

  • Made for BaseCamp on a Mac

    Libraries from BaseCamp for Windows may work, but they haven’t been checked yet.
